# Primitive: reframe(problem)

**What:** Generate alternative formulations of the problem that open different search spaces.

**Why:** The way a problem is stated constrains what solutions feel natural. Reframing changes the entire landscape before any ideas are generated.

## Techniques

### HMW (How Might We)

Rewrite the problem as 3-5 "How might we..." questions at different abstraction levels:
- **Narrow HMW:** focuses on a specific pain point
- **Broad HMW:** zooms out to the underlying need
- **Inverted HMW:** flips the problem (how might we make this worse? then invert)
- **Analogical HMW:** borrows framing from another domain

Example:
```
Problem: "Users abandon our onboarding flow at step 3"

Narrow HMW:   "HMW simplify step 3 so it takes <30 seconds?"
Broad HMW:    "HMW help users feel competent before asking for commitment?"
Inverted HMW: "HMW make step 3 so frustrating everyone quits?" (then invert)
Analogical:   "HMW make onboarding feel like unboxing a gift, not filling out tax forms?"
```

### Constraint Flip

1. List 3-5 assumed constraints
2. For each: "If we removed [constraint], what becomes possible?"
3. For each: "If we doubled [constraint], what would we be forced to do?"

### Starbursting

Generate questions (not answers) across: Who? What? Where? When? Why? How?
Aim for non-obvious questions that haven't been answered yet.

## Output

Present all framings. Ask steering team which 1-2 to pursue. The choice of framing IS the creative act.
